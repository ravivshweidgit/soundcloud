Cinematic film still, 16:9, photographic realism, 35mm film grain, natural light. Early morning at the end of summer in the western Negev, Israel. An old low wall, about chest height, seen strictly frontal, square-on, filling the whole width of the frame like a document photograph. The wall is built of hand-made mud bricks, and every brick is made of a different earth: red hamra soil, pale yellow loess, dark brown clay, grey clay, white chalky clay, ochre sand — a patchwork of many different clays side by side. Thin dark cracks already run along the joints between the different bricks, between them and not through them. One deep jagged vertical crack runs from the top of the wall down to the ground at exactly 72 percent of the frame width from the left edge (right of centre), following the joints. Behind the wall: dry golden stubble and a line of eucalyptus trees on the horizon, pale clean morning sky. Static camera at eye level, centred, unflattering, no artistic angle. No people, no text, no graffiti, no flags, no symbols.
