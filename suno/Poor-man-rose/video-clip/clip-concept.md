# Poor man rose — video clip concept (OpenArt)

## The title

"Poor man rose" = **maitohorsma** (rosebay willowherb / fireweed), Finnish nickname
**rentunruusu**, "the tramp's rose" — the flower of ditches and waste ground, too common to
be worth anything, beautiful anyway. Its older name is **paloruoho**, fire-grass: it is the
first plant to colonise burned and broken ground.

That is the film in one plant. Hard ground first, bloom second — and the bloom only happens
*because* the ground was ruined.

## Logline

**No people in this film.** A pair of whooper swans, mated for life, is separated by an
arctic winter. When the light comes back they find each other and fly — and the flight
climbs, through the fireweed coast and up into a valley of lupine under black peaks.

## Why swans only

The two things most likely to wreck an AI-generated clip are human face drift between
shots and any visible human-to-animal morph. Removing the people removes both. What is
left is a pair of white birds as **lovers** — intimacy, flight, reunion — and landscape,
which is what the cover references are actually about.

It also gives the chorus a literal subject. "Hey beautiful, over the clouds, you fly" is
one swan watching the other — **absent in chorus 1, in frame beside it in chorus 2.**

This is **art, not a nature documentary.** The pair composition and emotion come first
(see `references/swans/art-pair-*.jpg`). Species bill detail is optional spice, never the
priority. The story licence is the whole film — take it and do not explain it.

## Season logic (the spine)

| Lyric section | Season | The pair |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | summer sky | one swan, very high and small — subject established, pair withheld |
| Verse 1 — "let the poor man roses **pray**" | deep winter | **apart.** Each alone, never in the same frame |
| Chorus 1 | summer | **both fly, still apart.** Same sky, intercut, never together |
| Verse 2 — "let the poor man roses **grow**" | thaw | converging — first shared frame, then the reunion on water |
| Chorus 2 | high summer | **together.** Wingtip to wingtip, the flight the song is about |
| Outro (wordless) | endless light | held in the lupine valley, pulling back |

The verses are near-identical except **pray → grow**. Use that: verse 2 repeats verse 1's
framings exactly, thawing. One word changed in the lyric, so let the world be the only
other thing that changed. It also halves the location work — each pair comes from one base
still, re-lit.

## Colour score

Colour is the second storyline. It runs on its own curve, independent of the cutting.

| Section | Colour |
|---|---|
| Intro | Full colour — summer sky, so the loss lands when it goes |
| Verse 1 | **True black and white.** One exception: the two-second lupine flash in shot 5 |
| Chorus 1 | Colour returns, but **cool and muted** — overcast, sea level, rain light |
| Verse 2 | **Green.** Bleeding in across the section, near-monochrome at 17, full green by 24 |
| Chorus 2 | **Magenta and violet.** Full saturation, the most colour in the film |
| Outro | Holds chorus 2, easing slightly as the shots widen |

Three stages, and they never overlap: **grey → green → magenta.**

## The bloom is withheld

Same logic as the rainbow. In the north the **green arrives first and the flowers come late** —
maitohorsma is a high-summer bloom, not a spring one — so the thaw is green and **carries no
flowers at all.**

That is also what the lyric asks for. Verse 2 is "let the poor man roses **grow**" — growing, not
blooming. If the verse already has flowers in it, chorus 2 has nothing left to deliver.

**Flower colour belongs to the choruses exclusively.** No magenta, no violet, not one bud, in
either verse. Verse 2 gets grass, shoots, leaves, meltwater and bare stalks.

Black and white does real work in the winter beyond mood. The dead fireweed stalks become
pure graphic black lines on white, the open water becomes ink, and a white swan on snow
almost disappears — which is the point of that whole section. A desaturated colour image
cannot do any of that as cleanly.

**LOCKED — generate winter in colour, grade to black and white in the edit.** Do not prompt
for monochrome. That is what makes the bleed in verse 2 possible at all, and it lets you
place the exact frame where colour returns. Prompting black and white directly locks it.
Shot 5 is the single winter clip that stays in colour.

## The flowers, in flight order

The flight **ascends**, and that orders the references. Higher and further north means a
later thaw, so climbing into the mountains means flying back into earlier summer — which
is why the lupine can still be in flower at the end.

1. **Maitohorsma** (`cover-referenc-03.jpg`) — magenta on an open coast, cold sea, far
   islands. Chorus 1: lowland, sea level, alone.
2. **Maitohorsma** (`cover-referenc-02.jpg`) — magenta slopes, cobbled path, timber
   cottages, stormy mountain light. The middle altitude, on the way up.
3. **Lupiini + peaks** (`cover-referenc-01.jpg`, `cover-referenc-08.jpg`) — jagged black
   mountains above the flowers. The climb, not yet the arrival.
4. **Lupiini + snow peaks + double rainbow** (`cover-referenc-09.jpg` for layout, snow range
   and open sky; `NEK_4965.webp` for the storm and the two arcs) — **the destination.**
   Nothing comes after it except sky.

**Snow stays on the peaks in every summer shot.** Winter has not gone, it has only retreated
upward — the hard season is still visible in the frame that celebrates the bloom. That single
detail is the thesis of the film, so treat it as mandatory, not decoration.

Plant the lupine valley early as a two-second flash inside the winter verse, so the arrival
in chorus 2 is a return, not a surprise.

## The rainbow is withheld

"Over the rainbow skies" is sung eight times. It does not get its picture until the end.

- Intro (shot 3) and chorus 1 (shot 14): a **faint partial arc** in passing rain, incomplete.
- Chorus 2 and outro: the **full double rainbow**, at scale.

Two arcs for two birds — the double rainbow is a gate the pair can fly under, which is a
composition none of the other references offer.

A rainbow is only believable with rain and dark cloud behind it. Every rainbow shot therefore
needs a **squall on one side and low sun breaking from the other**, never a clear sky with an
arc pasted on. Reject any generation that returns a sticker rainbow.

## The reunion (the one beat that has to land)

Whooper swans have a real **greeting ceremony**: a reunited pair faces each other, necks
arched, wings half-raised, trumpeting together. That is the shot — it needs no invention
and no anthropomorphising, and it happens on the line "answered but not heard."

Its opposite is in verse 1: one swan raises its head and calls into the ice, and we cut to
the other with its head down, not hearing. The call *is* the answer, and it does not
arrive. Same gesture, winter and summer.

## Style prompt (paste base)

Nordic realism, arctic north, no people anywhere in frame. Winter half (graded to black and
white in the edit, so shoot for tone not hue): snow over charred broken ground, dead
fireweed stalks black against white, ice fog, black open water in white ice, high key,
minimal tonal range. Summer half: midnight sun low on the horizon, pale
gold and cold blue at once, never full darkness, long soft shadows, vast flower fields,
**snow still lying on the high peaks**, mountains and cold sea. 35mm film grain, natural
light only, wide lenses, slow or static camera. Melancholic turning to release.

## Avoid

People, hands, figures, silhouettes of humans — anywhere, including background. Any morph
or transformation. Swans as cartoon or mascot; keep them wild birds at real scale. Anatomy
errors in flight: prefer distance and silhouette over close wing detail. Tropical or
golden-hour-postcard light. Cars, buildings other than bare timber, text or burned-in
lyrics. Angels, fantasy, Viking imagery. Full darkness. Tidy gardens — this is a ditch
flower, keep the ground rough.

## Grace notes

- "Under midnight garden sun" sits inside the *winter* verse. Make it the **only colour frame
  in the black-and-white section**: two seconds of the lupine valley in full violet, then snap
  back to monochrome snow. Vision, not continuity error — and the single most violent cut in
  the film.
- Charred ground under the snow in verse 1, fireweed rising from that exact spot later.
  Pays off *paloruoho* without a word of explanation.
- A single swan on a shrinking patch of open black water is the whole winter in one image.
  White on white, the only dark thing being the water it cannot leave.

## Locked (production)

- Winter generated in colour → graded black and white in the edit (shot 5 stays colour).
- Swans only, no people.
- Transformation / reunion in chorus 2; chorus 1 is flight while still apart.
- Whooper swans; mythic north of the cover references; lupine island (ref 01) is the climax.
