# Poor man rose — shot list

Concept: `clip-concept.md`. Track 3:08 (188 s), 34 lyric lines. **Swans only, no people.**

**Production rule: one clip per lyric line, ~5.5 s each.** That is the natural edit rhythm
and the native OpenArt clip length at once — 35 clips total.

Section times are proportional estimates. Confirm against the audio before rendering; the
intro and outro probably run longer than an even split.

Every prompt inherits the base style block from `clip-concept.md`. Only the differences are
written here.

**Four standing rules:**
- **No human figure in any frame**, including background.
- **Snow stays on the high peaks in every summer shot** — winter retreated upward, it did not leave.
- **The full double rainbow is withheld** until chorus 2. Earlier rainbows are faint partial arcs only.
- **Low flight is the default.** See below.

## Low flight is the default

Every flying shot is **low — wingtip height, just above the flower heads or the water** — unless
it is explicitly marked AERIAL.

Speed and scale only exist near the foreground. At altitude nothing moves relative to the frame,
so flight reads as a hover and the lupine flattens into a purple stain; at flower height the
spikes rip past, the bird has a size, and you feel velocity. It also matches the song, which is
one bird speaking to one other bird, not a god's-eye view of a landscape. And practically, the
motion blur of fast low flight hides wing anatomy errors that slow high glides expose.

**Height is allowed in exactly two places:** shot 28, the ridge crest where the valley must open
*below* them, and shot 33, the widest frame of the outro. Both are brief. Everything else is low.

Things only low flight can give you, so use them: wingtips brushing the flower tops, seed-down
and petals kicked up in the wake, the pair's shadow racing along the ground, reflections running
directly beneath them on water, flowers still trembling after they have gone.

## Intro — est. 0:00–0:17 · summer sky · **full colour**

Subject established, pair withheld. Ends on a hard cut to ice — and out of colour.

1. **"Hey beautiful, over the clouds, you fly"** — Above a solid cloud deck, low midnight sun
   raking the tops, slow forward drift. Empty.
2. **"Hey beautiful o h- oh - u - oh, oho"** — Higher. Cloud canyons, long shadows across the
   deck. **One swan crosses very high and very small**, silhouette only. Do not centre it.
3. **"Hey beautiful, over the rainbow skies"** — A **faint, incomplete** rainbow arc at the
   cloud edge, no ground beneath it. A fragment, not the full arc. Hold — the last colour for
   fifty seconds — then **hard cut to black and white.**

## Verse 1 — est. 0:17–1:07 · deep winter, "pray", the pair apart

**True black and white**, one exception at shot 5. High key, minimal tonal range, ice fog.
Dead fireweed stalks as pure black lines on white, open water as ink. A white swan on snow
nearly disappears — that is the section's whole idea. **The two swans never share a frame.**

4. **"Let the poor man roses pray"** — Low wide: field of dead frozen fireweed stalks bowed
   under snow, charred broken ground showing through in patches. Wind only.
   *Lock this framing — it returns in shots 17 and 34.*
5. **"Under midnight garden sun"** — **The only colour frame in the black-and-white section,
   and the hardest cut in the film.** Two seconds of the lupine valley (`plate-lupiinit.png` /
   `NEK_4965.webp`) in full violet summer, then snap straight back to monochrome snow in the
   same framing. A memory cut, never a dissolve. **No rainbow yet** — save it. Plants the ending.
6. **"Dear god please make me a bird"** — **Drone approach** (`test-D.mp4`): camera starts low
   over frozen dead fireweed stalks, flies forward to the dark open water, and arrives at **ONE
   white swan alone** near the ice edge. Small → closer. Quiet. Colour master; grade B&W in edit.
   The winter twin of shot 29 (C leaves them; D finds one).
7. **"So I can fly far away"** — Its eyeline: empty white horizon, black peaks barely legible
   through fog. Nothing in the sky.
8. **"Where I like to fly, I fly with you I find,"** — **The other swan, elsewhere:** a
   different frozen bay, alone, tiny in a vast white frame. Different light so it reads as
   another place, not another angle.
9. **"find some mercy, find"** — Static detail: a dead fireweed stalk locked in ice, seed-down
   frozen to it, not releasing. *Returns in shot 22.*
10. **"Mercy in a land, o, yeah"** — Widest winter shot: whole valley under snow, one black
    open lead of water running through it like a scar. *Returns in shot 23.*
11. **"And what if every prayer, answered but not heard,"** — One swan raises its head and
    **calls into the ice**, breath visible. Cut: the other, head tucked down, not hearing.
    The call is the answer and it does not arrive. *Its opposite is shot 24.*
12. **"Fly far far away"** — Slow tilt from ice up to empty pale sky. Hold on nothing.

## Chorus 1 — est. 1:07–1:29 · both fly, still apart · **colour returns, cool and muted**

Colour comes back on the downbeat, but **overcast and cold** — sea level, rain light, nothing
like chorus 2's saturation. Magenta fireweed on an open coast (`cover-referenc-03.jpg`).
**They are both flying and they are not together** — intercut, never in one frame. The "you"
of the lyric is missing from the picture.

13. **"Hey beautiful, over the clouds, you fly"** — LOW. One swan running across open water and
    lifting off, feet slapping, spray, wings hauling hard. Camera low and close at water level.
    Magenta fireweed on the banks behind.
14. **"Hey beautiful, over the rainbow skies"** — LOW, fast. It flies alone just above the
    fireweed along the coast, flower heads ripping past the lens, headland and cold sea beyond,
    a **faint partial rainbow** in passing rain. Side-track, moving with it.
15. **"Hey beautiful, over the clouds, you fly"** — LOW. The other swan, also alone, also
    airborne, skimming a different coastline in different light. Same sky, different place.
16. **"Hey beautiful, over the rainbow skies"** — The one exception in this section: pull off the
    bird into a wide frame with **empty space beside it** where the second should be. The loneliest
    shot in the film. Cut away early, to meltwater.

## Verse 2 — est. 1:29–2:18 · the thaw, "grow", converging · **colour bleeding back**

**Verse 1's framings, shot for shot, thawing.** Colour returns gradually across the section:
nearly monochrome at 17, clearly green by 21, **full green by the reunion at 24.**
Cranes calling off-frame — heard, never seen.

**No flowers anywhere in this section.** The green comes first and the bloom comes late — that is
both botanically true in the north and what the lyric asks for ("**grow**", not bloom). Grass,
shoots, leaves, moss, meltwater and last year's bare stalks only. Not one magenta bud. Flower
colour belongs to the choruses.

17. **"Let the poor man roses grow"** — Shot 4's exact framing: snow retreating off the same
    ground, **green shoots** pushing up between last year's dead black stalks. Green only —
    no buds, no colour but green.
18. **"Under midnight garden sun"** — Shot 5's framing, now real and holding: sun low and not
    setting, long shadows on wet ground. No cut back to winter.
19. **"Dear god please make me a bird"** — Shot 6's framing: the ice is gone, the water is
    open, and **the swan lifts off from the exact spot it was trapped in.**
20. **"So I can fly far away"** — Ice breaking up, meltwater running hard, the valley
    draining. Motion everywhere after a static verse.
21. **"Where I like to fly, I fly with you I find,"** — **First shared frame:** two flight
    paths converging in a huge sky, both birds visible, still far apart.
22. **"find some mercy, find"** — Shot 9's stalk, thawed, finally releasing **last year's**
    seed-down into the air. Backlit, drifting. The old seed let go so the new growth can start.
23. **"Mercy in a land, o, yeah"** — Shot 10's widest valley, green and running with water,
    the black lead of ice now an open river.
24. **"And what if every prayer, answered but not heard,"** — **The reunion.** They land on
    the same water and perform the whooper greeting ceremony: facing each other, necks
    arched, wings half-raised, trumpeting together. Spray, noise, real bird behaviour.
    The answer to shot 11.
25. **"Fly far far away"** — Both run across the water and lift off **together.** Hold the
    climb into the chorus.

## Chorus 2 — **2:08–2:40 (31 s, confirmed against audio)** · together, the magical flight, ascending · **full saturation**

The flight climbs from the fireweed slopes to the lupine valley. `plate-maitohorsma.png` on the
way up, `plate-lupiinit-air.png` for the crest, `plate-lupiinit.png` for the arrival.
**This is the peak of the film.** Three of these four shots are low.

**Pacing — this section breaks the one-line-per-5.5s rule.** Four lyric lines across 31 s is
~7.75 s a line, half again the film's normal cut rate. Do not fill the gap with extra cuts: the
whole section is one continuous ascent, and cutting it up more would work against that. Spend the
extra time on the climb and the crest instead:

| Shot | Length | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 26 — water skim | ~7 s | normal |
| 27 — the climb | ~7 s | normal |
| 28 — AERIAL reveal | **~10 s** | the payoff of shot 5; it was always meant to hold |
| 29 — overtake to rainbow | ~7 s | normal |

Shot 28 getting the surplus is the point — it is the one frame the whole film has been
withholding.

26. **"Hey beautiful, over the clouds, you fly"** — LOW, wingtip to wingtip, skimming still
    water so close their reflections run directly beneath them and the wake spreads behind.
    The "you" is finally in frame beside the "I".
27. **"Hey beautiful, over the rainbow skies"** — LOW, climbing. The pair follows the rising
    line of the fireweed slope, magenta flower heads streaming past below them, bare timber
    cottages flicking by, stormy mountain light ahead. The ground stays close as it rises.
28. **"Hey beautiful, over the clouds, you fly"** — **AERIAL. The reveal, and the only high shot
    in the chorus.** They crest the ridge and the valley opens *below*: lupine to the horizon,
    snow peaks, the lake, the double rainbow full and complete for the first time. Payoff of the
    flash in shot 5. Use `plate-lupiinit-air.png`. Hold longer than the cut pattern — earn it.
29. **"Hey beautiful, over the rainbow skies"** — **Drop back down and go fast.** Camera starts
    behind the pair flying low over the lupine toward the rainbow, then accelerates, overtakes
    them on one side, and keeps flying alone as they fall behind and leave frame. Flower spikes
    stream past; the arch grows closer. Ends over the mirror lake with the rainbow doubled in
    the reflection. (Proven by `test-C.mp4`.) Anatomy-safe: we only ever see them from behind.
    Off-centre pass — never park them in the middle of the arch.

## Outro — est. 2:40–3:08 · wordless, endless light, pulling back

No story left. Hold; cut as little as possible. The section goes **wide once, then comes back
down** — it does not simply retreat to altitude, because ending on a distant dot throws away
everything low flight bought.

30. **"Hey beautiful, o h- oh - u - oh, o o"** — Open on the image Test C ended on: over the
    mirror lake, double rainbow reflected, snow peaks, **no birds**. The pair has already gone.
    Then continue the outro as planned — or cut straight to the wider frames.
31. *(same)* — LOW. Skimming the lake so close the wingtips almost touch it, reflections running
    beneath, the snow peaks and the arch standing behind them.
32. *(same)* — LOW and fast through the lupine, flower heads streaming past, the pair's shadow
    crossing the field beside them.
33. *(same)* — **AERIAL. The widest frame of the film** and the second of only two high shots:
    land, water and sky in thirds, the pair nearly lost in it. The diminishment. Hold.
34. *(same)* — **Return to shot 4's framing** — the charred winter ground, now deep in magenta
    fireweed under summer light. Camera low among the flowers. The pair crosses the far distance
    and **leaves frame.**
35. *(same, final)* — Stay exactly where 34 left us, low in the flowers, now **empty.** The
    fireweed is still trembling in the wake of something that has gone. It settles. The light does
    not change, because the sun is not going to set. Hold until the audio ends.

That last shot is the whole point of the title: the flower nobody valued is the thing still
standing when the beautiful thing has flown. Ending low, inside the flowers, makes that physical
— a distant wide shot would only make it decorative.

> **Alternative ending** — to close on the pair instead of the empty field, cut 35 and hold 34
> with the swans still in frame. You lose the trembling flowers, which is the better image.

## OpenArt workflow

1. **No character sheets needed** — that is the gift of dropping the people. Instead lock a
   **swan reference**: one approved still of the pair, reused as the base for every bird shot
   so size, posture and whiteness stay consistent.
2. **LOCKED — generate winter in colour, grade to black and white in the edit.** Do not
   prompt for monochrome. Grading in post is what makes the verse 2 bleed possible and lets
   you place the exact frame where colour returns; a monochrome source locks it forever.
   Shot 5 is the one winter clip that stays in colour.
3. **Keyframe stills, then image-to-video.** Approve a still per shot and animate from it.
   Prompting straight to video is where continuity dies.
4. **Generate season pairs together.** Shots 4/17/34, 5/18, 6/19, 9/22 and 10/23 are the same
   place in different seasons. Build each from one base still and re-light it rather than
   prompting the location twice from scratch.
5. **Test these two cheaply first:** shot 24 (does the greeting ceremony read, or does it come
   out as anatomy soup?) and shot 28 (does the lupine reveal actually land?). Those carry the
   film.
6. **Flight is the technical risk, and low flight is the mitigation.** Fast movement close to the
   foreground drags motion blur through the frame and hides wing errors; slow high glides expose
   them. Side-track with the birds rather than pointing at them. Avoid static close-ups of a
   working wing — that is where AI video falls apart.
7. 16:9 master; request 9:16 in the same render pass if a vertical cut is wanted.

## Shot budget

35 clips ≈ 190 s at 5.5 s. No slack — if a shot fails, cut it and hold a neighbour longer
rather than adding clips.
