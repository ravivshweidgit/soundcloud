# Man — character turnaround sheet prompt

For the Casting / Character Bible stage. Paste the block below the line.

## What was changed from Freebeat's draft

| Field | Was | Why it failed |
|---|---|---|
| Creative look | grey linen shirt blending into grey rock | he is always far and small — a grey figure on grey stone has no silhouette left |
| Creative look | rolled sleeves | bare forearms read warm and active; he is cold and motionless |
| Style | scene staging, time of day, and the woman | contradicts the white-studio lighting in the same prompt, and names a second person on a solo sheet |
| Hard constraints | hands unspecified | empty hands is what keeps *elevarás* from reading as *llevarás* |
| Identity | "statuesque posture" | posture is set by the mandated A-pose, not by identity |

---

TASK
Create one production-ready three-view character turnaround reference sheet
with Nano Banana 2 Lite for downstream storyboard and video conditioning.
This sheet is made by the film's own production, in the film's own medium.
STYLE is that medium: the person, skin, hair, cloth, light falloff, and finish
are rendered exactly the way the film renders things. The plain white ground
and neutral, even light are shooting conditions inside that medium — a
costume-fitting record made by this production — not a different world and not
a different medium. Identity decides who stands there; CREATIVE LOOK decides
what they wear.

INPUT ROLE
No identity image is attached for this text-only character.
Use the identity description below as the sole physical identity source.
[identity - preserve exactly]: Lean male figure in his late 30s, tall and
narrow, sharp angular jawline, short dark hair, pale skin tone, plain
weathered face, neutral expression.

SOURCE WARDROBE BOUNDARY
There is no source-image wardrobe authority. Physical identity text is not
wardrobe direction. The CREATIVE LOOK below is the only source of truth for
clothing, footwear, accessories, grooming, materials, colors, layers, and silhouette.

CREATIVE LOOK
Long charcoal wool overcoat falling to the knee, worn open and unbuttoned,
hanging straight and uninterrupted. Dark charcoal trousers. Dark scuffed
leather boots. Plain dark shirt beneath the coat, buttoned at the wrist, full
length sleeves, never rolled. One flat dark matte tone throughout, so the whole
figure reads as a single unbroken dark vertical. No hat, no scarf, no gloves,
no bag, no jewellery, no visible belt, no patterns, no prints, no logos.

STYLE
Filmic live-action photography. Naturalistic skin and cloth, soft light
falloff, matte finish, muted desaturated palette, organic 35mm film grain.
Not illustration, not painting, not 3D render, not stylized.

SHEET LAYOUT
Create one wide 16:9 image containing exactly three full-body depictions of the
same person. Arrange them left to right as front view, strict 90-degree left
profile, and back view. Use identical scale, equal spacing, and one shared
ground line. Show the entire head, hands, legs, and shoes in every depiction.
Use a neutral A-pose with relaxed hands, a neutral expression, and an eye-level,
orthographic-like presentation. Keep the three depictions visually separate;
do not overlap them or turn them into panels.

BACKGROUND AND LIGHTING
Use a pure solid white (#FFFFFF) background edge to edge, including the spaces
between views. Use soft neutral studio lighting and consistent exposure. Allow
only a subtle contact shadow directly beneath each pair of feet. The result is
one clean character reference image, not an environment or a designed page.

HARD CONSTRAINTS
- Treat all three depictions as the same person at the same moment.
- Exactly one person appears on this sheet. No second figure of any kind.
- Both hands are empty, open, and visible in every view. He carries, holds,
  and wears nothing in his hands.
- The coat stays open and unbuttoned and the sleeves stay full length in all
  three views.
- Preserve facial-landmark relationships, apparent age, skin tone, body build,
  height impression, hairline, hairstyle, hair length, parting, color, texture,
  and distinctive identity cues across all three views.
- Keep anatomy, outfit, footwear, accessories, materials, colors, seams,
  closures, and wear state identical across all views. Make side and back
  construction physically consistent with the front.
- Do not derive clothing from the physical identity description.
- Output exactly three views. Do not add a fourth view, 3/4 view, mirrored
  duplicate, face inset, head inset, close detail, action pose, cropped body,
  extra person, or standalone prop.
- Do not add scenery, furniture, a floor horizon, a cyclorama curve, gradient,
  texture, color tint, text, labels, arrows, measurements, swatches, borders,
  logos, captions, signatures, or watermarks.
