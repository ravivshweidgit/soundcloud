# New Hope — video clip concept (OpenArt)

Track: `New-hope.wav` / `New-hope.mp3` — **3:57 (237 s)**
Lyrics (sung): `../lyrics-suno.md`
Original: `../lyrics.md` (13.10.2014)
Style as rendered: `../styles-celtic-pop.md` — modern Celtic pop, **two** female Irish voices,

This file is the story. Workflow: `openart-guide.md` · Prompts: `openart-paste.md`

**The one-line version:** one woman waits in a live-action Irish glen; behind her door is a drawn
world in full cherry blossom; she takes the long way round and the camera goes through.

---

## The title

"New hope" is **morning**, not rescue. The situation has been waiting. The sun comes up on it anyway.

The two oldest lines in the song are the whole film:

> not forgotten / silence of hope

What follows is a look, a door, a road that ends, a sign (winter *or* blossom), then a river walk whose footprints prove someone was here.

And "silence of hope" is the reason the film needs two mediums. Hope that stays silent has to be
*shown* somewhere other than the world it is silent in.

## Logline

**One woman. One Irish glen. One night becoming morning — and one door that opens into a drawn world.**

She looks into the camera — into us — as if into a soul. We never see the singer. The second person exists as a closed door, a hundred steps of lane, and footprints on wet stones. Hope is the light arriving in her eyes before it arrives in the sky.

**The film is shot in two mediums.** The waiting world is live action: stone, cold rain, pre-dawn blue. Behind the door is animation: the **same Irish glen** in full wild-cherry blossom, sun and rain together. The door is not a prop; it is the border between waiting and hoped-for, and the whole film is built around one passage through it.

## Why one face, not two

The last two films taught opposite lessons:

- *Como la mano* — two people is the ceiling; same-frame faces drift; keep them apart by geography.
- *Poor man rose* — drop the people and you lose eyes. That song could survive it. This one cannot.

This lyric is made of eyes. "Sun is in your eyes / you are looking into my soul / eyes are like a mirror." So we lock **one woman** and let the camera be the lover. She looks into lens. We never cut to a second face.

**Two voices, one face — and that is the point.** The record has a lead and a close harmony, no third wordless voice. Two singers on one look is the same idea as one woman looking into a lens that is the other person: the second presence is *heard* and never *seen*. Do not cast the harmony as a second woman on screen. The harmony is her, doubled — or it is us.

## Two mediums — the door is the border

Live action is the world that waits. Animation is the world that is hoped for. The lyric already
splits exactly there, and on one word:

> Waiting for a **"winter day"**, / **Or** Cherry blossom through the rain

Winter day is live action — **cold rain on bare ground.** Cherry blossom through the rain is
**the same glen drawn** — wild cherry (*gean*), sunshower, more beautiful than a photograph.
**The word "Or" is the cut.** Same place; medium and season change.

### Two ways past a door

This settles the door question instead of contradicting it:

- **She goes around the gable** — the real way, on foot, in live action, on chorus 3. A hundred steps, an end of the road.
- **We go through the door** — the camera passes through it, and comes out in animation.

Two answers to *"asking where do we go?"*. She takes the long way round in the real world. We take
the impossible way through. The door is never a thing she opens; it is a thing **we** open.

### Why she is not in the animated world

The camera is the other person — the "you". The dream belongs to the "I": *"I find some peace
inside my dreams I saw"*. So passing through the door puts us **inside her dream, looking out of
it**, which is precisely why she cannot be standing in it. Her absence is the point of view.

That is also the cheap answer to the hardest technical problem. An animated version of a
live-action locked face is a consistency nightmare squared. **She never appears animated.** One
character lock, live action only. The drawn world is a place, not a cast.

### Blossom as animation of this glen

Drawn, the sunshower does what a camera cannot: rain as graphic lines on the **still**, petals as
strokes in motion, sun and wet air at once. The world behind the door is **this** cottage, lane,
river and cherry — redrawn — so it reads as a dream of *here*, not an escape to elsewhere.

**LOCKED — rain is filmed on the water, not in the air.** The video model cannot draw convincing
drops falling through open air, but it renders **impact** well: rings spreading on a puddle, the
surface dimpling and breaking, drips off wet stone. So every live rain shot is composed around
standing water — puddles, flooded ruts, the river — and the prompt describes the surface rather than
the sky. In the drawn world the rain stays painted on the still (`drawn-lane` already has its
streaks) and the video animates **petals and light only**. Rain overlays in the edit are a fallback
now, not the plan.

**Celtic-painterly, not anime, not sakura Japan.** Hand-drawn 2D, gouache and watercolour, paper
tooth, knotwork logic in the branches, gold leaf for stars. Wild cherry (*gean*), white blossom,
not candy-pink park sakura. No Ghibli pastiche, no 3D, no cel-shaded gloss.

### The medium plan

**One continuous animated block, not scattered inserts.** Bridge 1 is the drawn sequence. Bridge 2
is the same sequence in live action. Everything else is live action.

| | Bridge 1 — drawn | Bridge 2 — live action |
|---|---|---|
| Blossom | Full bloom painted; rain on the still; video = petals + light only | Real wild cherry in a real sunshower; rain read as lit rings on a puddle |
| River | Drawn water, gold-leaf stars on it | Cold dark water, night-blue |
| Stones | Graphic, weightless, untouched | One pressed under her bare foot, then taken by his hand |
| Footprints | **None** — nobody has walked in a dream | **Boot prints, there already, and not hers** |
| Stars | Gold leaf, decorative, dreamt | Withheld to the outro |

So the dream is the beautiful version and the real world is the one with evidence in it. The
footprints only mean something because the dream had none.

### Three passages, three mechanics

**Seed it first.** One second, inside verse 2: the gap under the closed door, or a keyhole, with a
sliver of impossible drawn colour coming through it. Same trick as the lupine flash in *Poor man
rose* — it makes bridge 1 a return rather than a surprise.

**In (bridge 1, on "Or").** Camera pushes at the door and through. Two acceptable endings:

1. **Into black** — safest for a clean medium cut; nothing has to match.
2. **Into a dark interior with one small window** — more realistic. Dim room, single window,
   cold light only, no furniture tour, no second person. The medium cut then happens on a
   **hard cut from that window glow (or the last dark frame) straight to `@drawn-lane`** —
   light answering light, or dark jumping to blossom. Do not dissolve. Do not invent a full
   lit kitchen.

If the interior resolves into a lived-in room, reject and re-roll toward darker.

**Out (into the eyes return).** Do not reverse the push — it would read as undoing. Instead the
drawn petals falling become real sleet falling, and we are back in live action mid-fall. Dream
into weather. The medium changes while one thing keeps happening.

**The last frame is both.** The outro returns to her live-action eyes, and the stars in them are
**drawn** — gold leaf on a photographic iris. The only frame in the film where the two mediums
share the screen. *"Then all the stars can shine bright in your dreams"* — the dream finally
reaching into the real world, one image, held.

### What this costs

Two style locks instead of one, and two sets of plates. Budget for it. The saving is that the
animated world is one sequence in one place with no character in it, so it is a style problem, not
a continuity problem.

## The glen (one geography, reused)

Same place in every weather, so the eyes verse can return in the same frames the way *Poor man rose* returned winter to thaw.

| Place | What the lyric needs |
|---|---|
| Her face, close | eyes, soul, mirror |
| Stone cottage, closed door | "waiting just behind the door" |
| A long lane of wet stone — countably long | "hundred steps away from you" |
| Where the lane dies at a gate or a wall | "end of the road" |
| The river below the lane, pebbles, dark water | bridge 2 — the real walk |
| The same glen, bare, raining | "winter day" — the lane's puddles held large, rain landing on them |
**The cherries are landmarks, not dream inventions.** The glen holds six to eight scattered wild
cherries — the big one beside the lane, a few along the river and in the field corners — and every
live plate shows them **bare**. Drawn blossom in bridge 1 and photographed blossom in bridge 2 are
those same trees in flower, in the same places. If the live glen has no trees, the drawn world is
inventing a place and "same glen" collapses.

**The wide valley is the live-action payoff.** Chorus 1 opens on the whole glen with every cherry
bare; bridge 2 pulls back to that identical frame with all of them in flower. One frame, twice,
with the film in between.
| **The drawn glen** — same geography, painted | bridge 1, behind the door |

**The drawn world is the same place, not a new one.** Same lane, same river, same cherry, same
shape of hill — redrawn. That is what makes it a dream *of this place* rather than an escape to
somewhere else, and it means the animation can be built from the approved live-action plates.

Wild cherry (*gean*) is native and does grow in Irish hedges, so the live-action tree in bridge 2
stays honest. Nothing Japanese in either medium.

No village, no pub, no castle, no harp-in-shot, no standing stones as tourist Ireland. Cottage, lane, river, weather.

## Sung form (locked)

The clip follows the block in `../lyrics-suno.md` — the paste you just confirmed — not the shorter 2014 page.

Nine sections. Eyes verse twice. Door verse once. Two different chorus openings. One bridge sequence, played twice.

```
Verse 1 (eyes) → Chorus 1 (morning sun)
Verse 2 (door / hundred steps) → Chorus 2 (behind your door)
Bridge 1 (winter/blossom/stars → river/pebbles/footprints)
Verse 1 return (eyes again) → Chorus 3 (morning sun)
Bridge 2 (same bridge, paid off)
Outro (stars)
```

The second `[Verse 2]` tag in the paste is the record's own label. In the picture it is **Verse 1 coming back**, not the door verse again.

### Two chorus openings — do not mix them up

| | Opening line | Picture job |
|---|---|---|
| Chorus 1 | "New hope rising / like this morning sun with you" | First gold. Sun **around her**, disk withheld. |
| Chorus 2 | "New hope rising just behind your door" | The door is the subject. Still closed. |
| Chorus 3 | "New hope rising like this morning sun with you" | Same words as chorus 1 — so the *world* has to have changed. Disk allowed. She can move. |

### Each bridge is two movements, not two different bridges

Both times, in this order:

1. **The wait** — winter day *or* cherry blossom through the rain → then stars in dreams
2. **The walk** — river → pebbles and stones → footprints from a midnight walk

First time through = **the dream, drawn.** It sits right after "inside my dreams I saw I told you",
so the animation is motivated by the lyric that precedes it. Winter day in live action, then the
passage through the door on "Or", then the drawn sunshower and the drawn river. No footprints.

Second time through = **real, live action.** The same beats with a camera instead of a brush.
Blossom in actual rain. Bare feet on cold pebbles, and a hand that is not hers taking the stone she stepped on. Footprints that are **not hers**, already in the mud.
Stars leave the dream and wait for the outro.

Same framings both times, same order. The medium and the evidence are what change.

## Section spine

**M** = medium. Live = live action, Drawn = animation.

| Section | M | Lyric hook | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verse 1 | Live | not forgotten / sun is in your eyes / mirror / where do we go | Pre-dawn. Extreme close on her eyes. Sun is **in them** (catchlight, not the disk). She looks into camera. Still. |
| Chorus 1 | Live | like this morning sun with you / hopes are sailing / understand it too | Pull back. First gold on the lane. She is small. **Sun disk withheld.** Light only. |
| Verse 2 | Live | behind the door / hundred steps / end of the road | Closed door. Then the lane, stepped off, toward the dead end. **The seed:** one second of drawn colour in the gap under the door. |
| Chorus 2 | Live | just behind your door | Door still the subject. Light hits it. Camera begins to approach it. **Does not open yet.** |
| Bridge 1 — wait | Live → **Drawn** | winter day **or** cherry through the rain / stars in your dreams | Bare glen in cold rain, live, grey. On **"Or"** the camera pushes through the door into black — and comes out **drawn**: sunshower, full blossom, painted rain. Gold-leaf stars. |
| Bridge 1 — walk | Drawn | river flow / pebbles and stones / footprints / midnight walk | The drawn river, same geography redrawn. Stones graphic on the bed. **No footprints — nobody has walked in a dream.** |
| Verse 1 return | **Drawn → Live** | same words as Verse 1 | Hard cut out of the last drawn petals into live wet air over the lane, then Verse 1's exact frames, **warmer**. Morning in the eyes for real. |
| Chorus 3 | Live | like this morning sun with you | **Sun disk allowed.** She comes around the gable into the lane. Camera travels. First chorus that moves with her. |
| Bridge 2 — wait | Live | same lines as bridge 1 wait | The real versions: cold rain on bare ground again, then **a real wild cherry in a real sunshower**, held, not flashed. Then the pull back to chorus 1's wide valley with **every cherry in flower**. No stars yet. |
| Bridge 2 — walk | Live | same lines as bridge 1 walk | Opens on the **same wide valley in blossom under a full moon**, then descends to the river. **She steps barefoot onto the pebbles**, pressing one distinct stone into the grit; **an unknown man's hand lifts that same stone** and is gone. Then **boot prints already there, not hers.** She finds them. |
| Outro | Live **+ Drawn** | oh / then all the stars can shine bright in your dreams | Back to her eyes. The stars in them are **drawn** — gold leaf on a photographic iris. The only frame where both mediums share the screen. Hold to the end. |

## The arrangement is the edit plan

The style spec puts the dynamics in the arrangement, so the cutting does not have to invent
them. **96 BPM 4/4 → one bar = 2.5 s**, so cut on bars: a 2-bar shot is 5 s, a 4-bar hold is 10 s.

| Arrangement | Section | What the camera does |
|---|---|---|
| Intimate harp and guitar, almost no drums | Verses | Static. Close. Long holds — 4 bars or more. She does not move. |
| Anthemic, wide radio mix, whistle + fiddle countermelody | Choruses | Wider frames, more air, shorter shots. Chorus 3 is the only one that travels. |
| **Fiddle-led** | Bridges | The fiddle takes the lead from the voices, so the *place* takes over from the face: the glen, the weather, the river. Least face in the film. |
| Whistle and close harmony | Outro | No cuts if possible. One held frame. |

Two useful consequences. The verses are sparse, so a static frame will not feel empty — it will
feel like the mix. And the bridges are instrumental-led, which is exactly why the footprints and
the two weathers live there: it is the one place the film can leave her face without a hole.

The tin whistle hook is the film's punctuation. Where it enters, let a cut land with it.

## Colour / light score

Light is the second storyline. It runs independently of the cuts, same as *Como la mano*'s cold valley → sunrise.

| Section | Light |
|---|---|
| Verse 1 | Blue-grey pre-dawn. The only gold is the catchlight in the eye. |
| Chorus 1 | First rim of gold. Cool shadows still win. |
| Verse 2 | Cool, the door in shade. Gold only on the top edge of the stone. |
| Chorus 2 | Warmer on the door. Interior still dark. |
| Bridge 1 | Live winter rain = grey, almost no gold, bare trees. Then **drawn**: painted sun *and* rain at once, pink and white blossom, gold leaf. The most saturated part of the film, and it is not photographic. |
| Verse 1 return | Gold in the iris. The face is lit from the window now. |
| Chorus 3 | Full morning. Warmest **photographed** frame in the film. |
| Bridge 2 | Night-blue on the river. Real rain on the cherry, cool and wet — deliberately less beautiful than the drawn version. |
| Outro | Live face, dark surround, drawn gold stars. |

Three stages, and they should not overlap until the eyes return: **blue → gold on stone → gold in the face.**

**The drawn world sits outside that curve on purpose.** It is brighter and more saturated than
anything the camera is allowed to produce, which is how the audience knows it is the hoped-for
world and not the real one. The live-action peak in chorus 3 must stay *below* the drawn peak in
bridge 1 — real morning should feel true, not triumphant.

## What is withheld

Same logic as the rainbow and the lupine.

1. **The animation** — one second seeded under the door in verse 2, then withheld until "Or" in bridge 1. Nothing drawn before the seed.
2. **The passage through the door** — the camera does not go through until bridge 1. Chorus 2 may approach it and stop.
3. **The sun disk** — in her eyes first, in the sky only in chorus 3.
4. **Real blossom** — bridge 2 only. Every live cherry is bare until then. The drawn blossom comes first, the photographed one second, so the real trees answer the dream instead of previewing it. The whole valley in flower is held back to the very end of bridge 2.
5. **Footprints** — none in bridge 1 (a dream has no evidence). Bridge 2 only. **They are boot prints and she is barefoot**, so "not hers" reads on screen instead of only in the brief.
6. **The hand** — the second person is withheld all film and then arrives as **one hand only**, in bridge 2, to lift the single pebble she has just stepped on. No face, no body, no reflection, one appearance. She touches the stone; he takes the thing she touched; they never meet. In the painted dream nobody picks anything up at all — that absence is what makes the hand land.
6. **Her walking** — verses she is still. Choruses 1–2 only the *world* moves. **She** walks in chorus 3, around the gable, and on the real river.
7. **The two mediums in one frame** — never, until the last shot. Drawn stars in a live eye is the final image and nothing before it may share a frame.

If chorus 1 already has the disk, chorus 3 has nothing to deliver. If bridge 1 already has the
footprints, bridge 2 is only a repeat. If the two mediums touch before the outro, the last frame
is just another composite.

## Two style bases (paste bases, later)

Two locks, one per medium. Never mix the two blocks in one generation except the final frame.

### A — live action (everything except bridge 1)

Irish west, live-action film, natural light, 35mm grain, matte. One woman only, late twenties to early thirties, soft gentle freckled face, natural copper-auburn red hair — not weathered, not harsh. Pre-dawn blue moving to wet morning gold. Stone cottage, long wet lane, dark river, cold rain on bare ground, one wild cherry in real rain. Intimate verses, wider choruses. Hopeful, not pretty-postcard. Avoid: animation or illustration look, second face, lip sync, on-screen lyrics, fantasy, angels, pub, castle, musicians or instruments in shot, tourist Ireland, warm fake sunset, text.

### B — drawn world (bridge 1 only)

Hand-drawn 2D animation, gouache and watercolour on paper, visible paper tooth and brush edges, painted light. The same Irish glen redrawn: lane, river, one great cherry tree in full blossom, hills beyond. A sunshower — bright sun and falling rain at once, rain as drawn lines, petals as brush strokes, wet gleam on everything. Celtic ornament in the details: knotwork logic in the branches and roots, gold leaf for the stars on the water. Luminous, saturated, more beautiful than a photograph could be. No people, no animals, no text. Avoid: anime or manga style, sakura park, Ghibli pastiche, cel-shaded gloss, 3D render, CGI, photorealism, live-action plates, vector flatness, comic panels, outlines drawn in pure black.

**No instruments in frame, ever, in either medium.** The record is full of harp, whistle and
fiddle, and the temptation is to show them. A visible player makes the film a performance video
and puts a second person in the world. The instruments are the score; the picture stays her, the
glen and the drawn glen.

## Avoid (production)

- A second person anywhere in frame, including background and reflections (the mirror is her eye, not a second body).
- **Her, animated.** She exists in live action only. No drawn woman, no drawn silhouette, no figure in the blossom.
- Lip sync — the song is already recorded. She looks; she does not perform.
- Walking in close-up (AI gait). Walk only wide, or not at all until chorus 3, and keep it distant.
- Tight hand-and-stone close-ups that melt fingers. Prefer the stones in a wide palm, or already on the ground.
- Anime sakura, torii gates, kimono, koi, lanterns. The drawn world is Celtic and it is the same glen.
- A drawn world that is a *different place*. Same lane, same river, same hill line, redrawn.
- Green novelty Ireland: leprechaun palette, claddagh jewellery as costume.
- Dissolves and cross-fades between mediums. The changes are: through black, on a falling object, or a hard cut. Never a soft mix.

## Locked

**Story**

- One woman, one glen, camera = the other person. **Soft gentle face, natural redhead** (not the earlier weathered grey-streak look).
- The harmony voice is never a second woman on screen.
- Sung form = the paste in `../lyrics-suno.md`. Eyes twice, door once, bridge twice.
- **Two mediums. Live action waits, animation is hoped for. The door is the border.**
- **She goes around the gable; the camera goes through the door.** She never opens it, and she never appears drawn.
- **Bridge 1 is drawn, bridge 2 is the live-action answer to it.**
- **Cold rain first (bare), then blossom in rain** — the wait, then the thing that feels like hope. Both bridges. Winter day = rain, not frost.
- **Verse 1 is eyes only.** No context until chorus 1 pulls back.
- **Last frame: her live-action eyes with drawn gold-leaf stars in them.** The only frame with both mediums.

**Production**

- 16:9 master. 9:16 later if needed.
- Stills first, then image-to-video. Character lock before any environment plate.
- Two style locks, A and B, approved separately before any plate work.
- Animation is one continuous block plus the one-second seed and the final stars — not scattered inserts.
- **Rain is filmed on the water.** Compose every live rain shot around standing water and prompt the surface — rings, dimpling, drips off stone — never drops in open air. Drawn rain stays painted on the still; drawn video animates petals and light only.

## Still open

1. **The drawn style, precisely.** "Gouache and watercolour with Celtic ornament" is a direction, not a lock. It needs one approved still to become real — and it is the one asset with no precedent in the other two films.
2. **How far does the Celtic ornament go?** Ornament living quietly in the branch structure, or a real illuminated-manuscript language with borders and gold grounds? The second is more original and much easier to make look cheap.
3. **Does the drawn world move the camera?** Drawn plates with a slow push are safe; drawn *animation* with moving petals and rain is the whole point but is the most likely thing to fail in OpenArt. Worth a cheap test before committing.
4. **Is the seed under the door, or through the keyhole?** Under the door is a graphic line of light and easier to draw. A keyhole is a better metaphor and a worse image.
5. **Does the bare rain in bridge 2 repeat bridge 1's rain shot exactly?** Exact repeat makes the pairing legible; a new angle makes it less mechanical.

Questions 1–3 are settled by generating, not by deciding: `openart-paste.md` has two drawn-style
candidates, A (ornament inside the painting) and B (illuminated manuscript). Pick on the evidence.

Order of work is in `openart-guide.md`. Nothing in Phase 1a or 1b depends on the questions above,
so generation can start now: character sheet → face lock → the two drawn candidates.
